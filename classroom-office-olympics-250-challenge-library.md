# 250-Challenge Library for Classroom & Office Olympics

This library expands the original product spec with 250 curated challenge ideas for classroom and office events. The challenge formats are inspired by common playful dare, team-building, and gamified activity patterns found across educational, workplace, and youth-oriented social content, but are rewritten into original, safer challenge cards for app use.[cite:24][cite:25][cite:27][cite:32][cite:45][cite:65]

The library is designed so hosts can rely primarily on built-in content rather than creating their own challenges. It also supports points, tournament rounds, team play, and moderation-friendly filtering by tone, intensity, movement, and noise level.[cite:42][cite:49][cite:57]

## Library fields

- ID: Unique challenge identifier.
- Audience pack: Classroom Funny, Classroom Low-Noise, Office Fun, Office Professional, or Universal Crowd-Pleasers.
- Category: Main interaction type.
- Intensity/Tone: Mild, Bold, Chaos, Legend for classroom-style content; Social, Balanced, Professional for office content.
- Duration: Suggested round time.
- Movement: Seated, Standing, or Light movement.
- Noise: Quiet, Medium, or Loud.
- Submission: Completion tap, judge score, vote, text, photo, or hybrid.
- Summary: Short mobile-friendly description.

## Suggested scoring rules

- Completion: 10 points.[cite:42][cite:57]
- Quality or correctness: +5 to +10 points depending on challenge type.[cite:49][cite:55]
- First finish bonus: +8 points.[cite:42][cite:49]
- Crowd favorite or judge bonus: +5 points.[cite:42][cite:57]
- Teamwork bonus: +3 points for strong collaboration in designated team rounds.[cite:49][cite:51]
- Rule breach or unsafe conduct: zero points or disqualification.[cite:28][cite:30][cite:33]

## Usage notes

This library should be treated as seed content for the app database. Each challenge can later be enriched with fuller instructions, banned actions, moderation flags, materials, age bands, and audience-safe wording before final production use.[cite:28][cite:30][cite:33]

Social platforms are useful for spotting styles young people find funny, but not for direct copying. The safest approach is to use social and community content for inspiration, then rewrite the patterns into original, moderated, app-ready challenge cards.[cite:23][cite:25][cite:65][cite:70]
